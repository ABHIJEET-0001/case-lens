# Time-to-Resolution Prediction & Synthetic Label Methodology

## 1. Motivation & Context
Standard legal NLP datasets for Indian appellate courts (such as ILDC and IN-Abs) contain extracted judgment full texts and final disposition labels (Allowed / Dismissed). However, judicial registry timestamps (filing date, initial hearing notice, and interim order dates) are not systematically tracked or standardized across High Court and Supreme Court e-Court archives.

To train and evaluate time-to-resolution forecasting models before real-time e-Court API scraping is authorized, CaseLens utilizes an empirical synthetic labeling calibration pipeline.

## 2. Calibration against the National Judicial Data Grid (NJDG)
Synthetic resolution durations ($T_{days}$) are calibrated against official statistics published by the **National Judicial Data Grid**:
- **Distribution**: Log-normal distribution with median $\mu = 1,120$ days and standard deviation $\sigma = 480$ days.
- **Complexity Multipliers**:
  - Constitutional / Full Bench appeals: $1.4\times - 1.8\times$
  - Standard Criminal Appeals (bail / suspension): $0.5\times - 0.7\times$
  - Commercial Arbitration challenges (Section 34): $0.8\times - 1.1\times$

## 3. Precedent Feature Interaction (Ablation Grounding)
The central empirical hypothesis tested in CaseLens is whether embedding-retrieved precedent signals improve time-to-resolution forecasting compared to metadata-only baselines.

The 6 extracted precedent features:
1. `top1_similarity`: Cosine similarity of the nearest semantic authority.
2. `mean_top5_similarity`: Average semantic agreement among top 5 authorities.
3. `count_above_threshold`: Number of retrieved authorities with $\text{sim} \ge 0.75$.
4. `similarity_spread`: Difference between top-1 and top-5 similarity.
5. `precedent_age_mean`: Elapsed years from precedent delivery to petition filing.
6. `outcome_agreement`: Proportion of top 5 precedents that share a common disposition (allowed vs. dismissed).
